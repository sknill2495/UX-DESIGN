## 001 · The Door That Makes You Look Stupid
**Category:** Product Experience · **Subject:** "Norman doors" (Don Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*) · **Story:** When you push a pull door, the door failed, not you.

**Psychology** — Watch: everyone has done this in public. · Surprise: there's a name for it and the blame flips to the designer. · Trigger: Relatability. · Share: "Ye toh tu roz karta hai." · Save: a first lesson in affordances. · Debate: worst door in your office/college?

**Strategy** — Hook: footage of someone pushing a "PULL" door. · Angle: blame reversal. · Insight: if a product needs a label to explain it, the design has already failed. · Duration: 45s · Visuals: POV door fails, then close-ups of a flat push plate vs a bar handle. · Scores: Engagement 9 · Share 9 · Save 6.
**Sources:** [Certain] Don Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things* (1988, rev. 2013). Vox explainer "It's not you. Bad doors are everywhere" (2016): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yY96hTb8WgI

**Script (45s)**
- **0–3 HOOK:** "Pull likha tha… aur aapne push kiya. Galti aapki nahi thi."
- **3–10 SETUP:** "Design ki duniya mein aise doors ka ek naam hai — *Norman Doors*. Don Norman ke naam pe, jinhone 'User Experience' term popular kiya."
- **10–25 STORY:** "Door ka handle khud bolta hai kya karna hai. Ek vertical bar haath ko pakadne ke liye bolta hai, toh hum *pull* karte hain. Ek flat plate haath ko dhakelne ke liye bolti hai, toh hum *push* karte hain. Problem tab aati hai jab push door pe bhi pull wala handle laga ho."
- **25–38 TWIST:** "Aur phir designer kya karta hai? Ek sticker laga deta hai — PUSH. Norman ka rule simple hai: agar ek simple cheez ko instruction label ki zarurat pad rahi hai, toh design fail ho chuka hai."
- **38–45 ENGAGE:** "Aapke office ya college mein sabse confusing door kaunsa hai? Photo comment karo — Hall of Shame banate hain."

## 002 · The Dropdown That Told a State a Missile Was Coming

**Strategy** — Hook: the actual alert text on a phone mock. · Angle: a disaster explained through UI. · Insight: high-stakes actions need friction, confirmation that differs from routine actions, and a fast undo. · Duration: 60s · Visuals: phone alert recreation, news headlines, a simplified mock of a list of links. · Scores: E 9 · S 9 · Sv 7.

## 006 · The Loading Bar That Makes You Trust the Result
**Category:** Product Experience · **Subject:** The "labor illusion" (Buell & Norton, 2011) · **Story:** When a website shows you it's working ("Searching 200 airlines…"), people value the result more, even if it takes longer.

**Psychology** — Watch: everyone sits through loaders. · Surprise: slower can feel better. · Trigger: Curiosity. · Share: designers and founders will forward it. · Save: something to use in your own product. · Debate: is a fake "working" animation honest?

**Strategy** — Hook: two screens side by side, an instant result vs an animated "searching" one. · Angle: unexpected comparison. · Insight: visible effort creates perceived value. · Duration: 45s · Visuals: screen recordings of travel search, a kitchen with an open view of the chef as a real-world parallel. · Scores: E 8 · S 7 · Sv 9.
**Sources:** [Certain] Buell & Norton, "The Labor Illusion: How Operational Transparency Increases Perceived Value," *Management Science* (2011). HBS working paper: https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=39978

**Script (45s)**
- **0–3 HOOK:** "Kabhi kabhi slow website, fast website se zyada trustworthy lagti hai."
- **3–10 SETUP:** "Flight search karte ho, screen pe likha aata hai 'Searching airlines…', aur ek bar chalta hai. Kya sach mein itna time lagta hai?"
- **10–25 STORY:** "Harvard ke researchers Ryan Buell aur Michael Norton ne isko test kiya. Jab website ne apna 'kaam' dikhaya, log result ko zyada value dene lage, chahe wait thoda lamba ho."
- **25–38 TWIST:** "Isse kehte hain *labor illusion*. Mehnat dikhti hai toh value lagti hai. Isliye restaurants mein open kitchen hote hain, aur isliye kuch apps result dene se pehle 'thinking…' dikhate hain."
