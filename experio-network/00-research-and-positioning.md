## 1. Executive summary

**The uncomfortable finding first:** almost none of Experio's topics are new. IKEA's maze, Norman doors, the decoy effect, Netflix thumbnails and the dabbawalas have all been covered by large channels. **The topic is not the moat.** Experio can own three things:

1. **Myth vs Mechanism, a fact-checking stance on famous business stories.** Our own research turned up that two of the most repeated stories are shakier than people think. The "Forbes certified the dabbawalas as Six Sigma" claim is denied by the dabbawala association's own leader, and error-rate figures vary between 1-in-6M and 1-in-16M depending on the source ([Wikipedia summary](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Dabbawala), [The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/article/what-harvard-learning-mumbai-dabbawalas)). The famous Houston baggage-claim story has no traceable primary source ([Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/read/baggage-claim-wait/)). Correcting a story people already believe gives them a reason to share it, and few short-form creators do this.
2. **Hinglish + an Indian parallel for every global story.** Think School owns long-form Indian business analysis. Built for Mars and growth.design own English, text-first UX analysis. Nobody clearly owns **60-second, Hinglish explainers on experience and UX psychology with an Indian parallel in every episode** (a gap I inferred from the creator map below).
3. **A consistent "experience lens."** Every episode asks the same question: *what did this feel like for the human, and why?* Business channels ask "how did they make money." Experio asks "what did it feel like, and who designed that feeling?"

**Platform guidance that actually matters (and what's unverified):**

**What this means for Experio:**
- Optimize for **sends**. Every script ends with a reason to send it to a *specific person* ("send to your parents," "tag the friend who…"), not "like and follow."
- Optimize for **the first 3 seconds** (swipe-away). Every hook is a visual + one spoken line. No logo intro, no "Hey guys."
- **Never re-upload ads or clips you don't own.** Describe iconic ads, recreate scenes, or use licensed stock. This protects the originality signal and avoids copyright strikes.
- Hashtags: use 3–5 descriptive ones. Searchable **keywords in captions and on-screen text** do more work than hashtags on both platforms.

**Patterns observed across these creators [Likely]:**
- Winning hooks **state a misconception or a number** in the first line ("You've been doing X wrong," "This $1.50 hot dog…").
- Recurring **series names** build return viewers (Fireship's "in 100 seconds," Company Man's "What happened to…").
- Indian creators who win in Hinglish rely on **one confident face on camera + B-roll + bold captions**. That's affordable and builds trust faster than faceless motion graphics.
- **Sources on screen** (paper title, news headline) are rising as a trust signal, especially in AI-era scepticism.

## 3. Audience insights & content opportunities

**Recurring interests and questions [Likely, check against real comments]:**

| Audience | What they consistently want | What they push back on |
|---|---|---|
| Curious general viewers | "Why is this like this?" and "I never noticed that" | Being lectured, long intros |
| Designers / UX / PMs | Named principles they can use at work (peak-end, defaults, affordances), real examples | Wrong citations, pop-psych without studies |
| Founders / marketers | Indian brand stories, pricing psychology, crisis comebacks | Survivorship-bias "success formula" content |
| Tech / AI enthusiasts | AI reality checks, what works vs hype | Uncritical hype or doom |
| Indian viewers broadly | Pride stories (UPI, dabbawalas, Aravind), nostalgia (Maggi, Amul, Paper Boat) | Western stories with no Indian relevance |

**Content opportunities, ranked:**
1. **Myth vs Mechanism** (fact-checking famous stories). Strongest point of difference. Sources: 016, 018, 029, 061, 082, 088.
2. **Dark Pattern Detective**. India's CCPA named 13 dark patterns in Nov 2023 and issued a self-audit advisory in June 2025 ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2023/12/223-consumer-protection-authority-dark-pattern-guidelines/), [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/from-nudge-to-notice-what-indias-dark-pattern-orders-mean-for-digital-business)). The FTC–Amazon $2.5B settlement (Sept 2025) gives the global hook ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/amazon-ftc-prime-settlement-2-5-billion)). These videos are high-save and high-send ("send to parents"), and they help viewers protect themselves.
3. **Made-in-India Experience**: UPI, soundbox, PIN codes, langar, Aravind. Evergreen and pride-driven.
4. **AI Reality Check**: Klarna's AI-then-humans arc ([Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-pursues-uber-style-customer-service-model/812763/)), the Air Canada chatbot ruling, Just Walk Out. The audience is tired of hype and doom and wants balanced takes.
5. **Psychology you can use**: decoy, anchoring, free trials, sunk cost. Highest save rates in the category, and they work as a regular weekly slot.

**Evergreen vs trend-linked:** about 80% of the 100 ideas are evergreen. Trend-linked ideas (091–100, 054, 072, 005) need re-verification the week you record.

## 4. Experio Network content positioning

**Positioning statement:**
> For curious Indians who want to understand the world they use every day, Experio Network is the Hinglish short-form channel that reveals *who designed the experience and why it works on you*. Unlike business-explainer channels that ask "how did they make money?", Experio asks "how did it make you feel, and who decided that?" And it fact-checks the famous stories everyone repeats.

**Brand pillars:**
1. **Experience lens.** Every episode names the human feeling first, then the system behind it.
2. **Evidence on screen.** A source card in every video. Mark claims honestly ("reported," "contested").
3. **India + world.** Every global story gets an Indian parallel, and every Indian story gets a global benchmark.
4. **Useful twist.** Every video ends with something the viewer can notice, use or protect themselves with.

**Voice:** a smart friend over chai. Hinglish, confident, slightly playful, never preachy. No "Did you know?" openers. No fake urgency.

**Visual identity (recommended):**
- One host on camera, mid-shot, natural light. Use a consistent background or a branded wall.
- **Source card** (bottom-left, 2 seconds): "Source: Science, 2003."
- **Confidence stamp** for myth episodes: TRUE / CONTESTED / MYTH.
- Two brand colours plus white captions. Caption style stays the same in every video.
- Sign-off line (spoken in under 2s or on-screen only): *"Understand the experience. Change the perspective."*
